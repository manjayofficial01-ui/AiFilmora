# F4: Filmora player controls and timeline tools

## Findings

### [1] Filmora 15 ships dual independent preview monitors named "Source Monitor" (raw media/assets) and "Timeline Monitor" (sequence output), marketed as "Source & Timeline Preview Editing."
- quote: "Filmora introduces a revolutionary dual-monitor system: the Source Monitor and the Timeline Monitor. The Source Monitor is dedicated to previewing raw footage and various assets (like effects and transitions) from your media library... The Timeline Monitor, on the other hand, displays the final edited result on your timeline in real-time"
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/dual-monitor-setup-editing.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown (current Filmora 15 guide)
- confidence: high

### [2] Source and Timeline monitors are floatable windows that can be dragged to a second screen; selecting a Media Library asset previews it in Source Monitor while timeline edits update Timeline Monitor in real time.
- quote: "When you select an asset from the media library, its preview appears in the Source Monitor. Dragging that asset to the timeline will show its final effect in real-time within the Timeline Monitor."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/dual-monitor-setup-editing.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [3] Filmora 15 monitor overlay options include Video Scopes and Aspect Ratio change from the dual-preview workflow.
- quote: "Additionally, you can make precise adjustments using options like the Video Scopes and change the Aspect Ratio as needed."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/dual-monitor-setup-editing.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [4] The Player Panel (Preview Window) exposes playback quality/display settings, Snapshot, and Mark In/Mark Out; the panel can be undocked and repositioned by dragging.
- quote: "The Player Panel (Preview Window) shows you how the video plays in the Timeline. This is where you can adjust different settings, such as: • Playback quality and display settings • Capture a snapshot of the video • Mark in and Mark out"
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/panel-layout.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [5] Snapshot sits under the timecode and writes a full-resolution still frame into the Media Library automatically.
- quote: "Underneath the time code is the Snapshot button. Snapshot lets you take a full-resolution still frame and automatically saves the picture to your Media Library as an image."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/panel-layout.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [6] Preview playback quality dropdown offers exactly Full, 1/2, and 1/4 resolution; reduced preview quality does not affect export, and Filmora may prompt to lower quality when it detects lag.
- quote: "Full: preview your video without changing the original resolution • 1/2: preview your video at 1/2 the original resolution • 1/4: preview your video at 1/4 the original resolution... The reduced playback resolution only applies to the video preview and will not affect the export settings."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/change-playback-quality-win.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [7] Project Settings (File > Project Settings) set aspect ratio, resolution, and frame rate for the project.
- quote: "Project settings include aspect ratio, resolution, and frame rate. You can adjust the parameters you want."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/panel-layout.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [8] Timeline track headers provide Eye (hide video track), Speaker (mute audio track), and Lock icons; the + / Manage Timeline control adds video/audio tracks and Adjust Track Height.
- quote: "Click the Eye icon on any video track to hide it from the preview... For audio tracks, click the Speaker icon to mute the sound instantly... click on the Lock icon to lock the track... Click the + icon located at the bottom left corner of the timeline to enable the Manage Timeline option. From here, choose Add Video Track or Add Audio Track"
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/use-the-timeline.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [9] Timeline panel supports delete, split, parameter edit, preview render, markers, voiceover record, and audio mix; view controls include Zoom to Fit Timeline and a zoom slider plus drag-on-ruler zoom.
- quote: "Delete video clips, audio, transitions, elements, etc. • Split your video clips or audio files. • Edit your media file's parameters. • Render your video for a preview. • Add marker. • Record voiceover. • Mix audio and more."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/panel-layout.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [10] Compound Clip acts as a nested timeline opened as a tab above the main timeline; Filmora 15 Dual-Timeline Editing supports tab switching, dual timeline view by dragging a compound tab down, and Export Current Timeline.
- quote: "Right click on the track and choose the Create Compound Clip option... Double-click it to open it as a separate timeline tab above the main timeline... Drag a compound clip tab down into the timeline area to activate dual timeline view."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/use-the-timeline.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [11] Split is available four ways: Split button on the playhead (splits all clips under playhead unless a track is selected), scissors icon above the timeline, right-click Split, and Quick Split Mode; the Split button can be enabled via File > Preferences > Editing.
- quote: "click the split button on the playhead to create the split... If you don't select a specific track in your timeline, you will split all of the clips under your playhead... If you didn't see the Split Button in the timeline, you can go to File -> Preferences -> Editing to show it."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/trim-split-cut.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [12] Trim paths: Mark In/Mark Out in the Preview window (pre-timeline subclip), edge-drag on timeline clips, and Trim Start to Playhead / Trim End to Playhead from the right-click Trim menu.
- quote: "In the Preview window, you can use the Mark In and Mark Out points to break down a video into multiple segments without importing it to the timeline first... select Trim Start to Playhead or Trim End to Playhead under Trim context menu"
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/trim-split-cut.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [13] Filmora distinguishes Media Markers (added to source media with Mark icon or M before timeline placement) from Timeline Markers (added in post via timeline toolbar Bookmark / Add Marker or M at playhead); markers can be renamed, commented, color-coded via Modify Marker.
- quote: "To add a marker to the media, first choose the media and then click the Mark icon or press M... From the timeline toolbar, select the Bookmark icon saying Add Marker... choose the Modify Marker option... name your marker, add a comment in the Comment box, and change the marker color."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/add-and-remove-marker.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [14] Freeze Frame is under the clip right-click Speed submenu as Add Freeze Frame; Filmora auto-splits the clip and inserts a still whose Duration is set via right-click Duration, and segmented speed Freeze Frame defaults to 5 seconds.
- quote: "Right-click on the video clip in the timeline, select Speed and choose Add Freeze Frame from the menu. Filmora will automatically split the clip and insert a still image at the selected frame."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/freeze-frame.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [15] Segmented Speed Control uses Speed > Add Speed Point to create independent speed segments with Slow/Normal/Fast or custom values, optional Freeze Frame between segments, and Clear Clip Speed to reset to 1x.
- quote: "Expand the Speed option and choose Add Speed Point... Use the preset options such as Slow, Normal, or Fast, or manually enter a custom speed value... Use the Freeze Frame option to automatically create a separate freeze segment with a default duration of 5 seconds."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/change-video-speed.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [16] Motion Tracking lives in the Properties panel Basic section: enable Motion Tracking toggle, Click to Start Motion Tracking, then Link a text/image/effect element to the tracked subject.
- quote: "head to the properties panel on the right and enable the Basic section. Locate the Motion Tracking feature and turn its toggle on. Press the Click to Start Motion Tracking option... expand the Link drop-down menu within the properties panel and select the element to be linked."
- url: https://filmora.wondershare.com/guide/motion-tracking.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

## Dead ends
- Google Search webfetch returned JS/captcha wall (Nepali error page); no SERP usable.
- Bing Search for "Timeline/Source tabs / safe areas / aspect ratio" returned completely unrelated Chinese security-software results (huorong.cn).
- No official Filmora documentation found for player "safe areas" / title-safe / action-safe overlays — either absent or undocumented in the Windows user guide.
- Label "Full Quality" not used in docs; the dropdown is documented as Full / 1/2 / 1/4 playback quality.
- Classic Premiere-style single-monitor Timeline|Source tab pair is not how Filmora 15 documents preview: it uses separate Source Monitor and Timeline Monitor windows (not tabs on one player).
- Dedicated pages for reverse, detach audio, compound-clip deep UI, silence detection params, and beat detection params were listed in TOC but not deep-fetched (budget); only TOC-level coverage for those.
